# Episode 001 pre-render review panel

Three distinct reviewers independently examined the complete focal texts, the
script, and the episode notes. Reviewers 1 and 2 required revisions; Reviewer 3
examined the already revised package. Every required revision was applied and
rechecked against the extracted full texts of all five sources. The findings
below are their genuine final decisions on the current files.

## Reviewer 1 — Quantitative accuracy and source attribution

**Final verdict: PASS.** Rendering is authorized by this reviewer.

Required revisions: the draft attributed the "42 countries, 70,000 participants"
figures to the 2018 Nature Moral Machine paper. These belong to the 2020 PNAS
paper, which used the site's Classic mode on Switch/Loop/Footbridge variants and
is a different instrument with a different sample. The Nature paper's figures are
~40 million decisions across 233 countries and territories on nine AV-dilemma
attributes. Conflating them would have misstated both studies.

Resolution: the two studies are now separated, cited independently as sources 2
and 3, and the script contains an explicit on-air correction turn distinguishing
them. The reviewer verified every number against the extracted texts: 76% and
n = 182 and CI 69–82 and medians 85/19/50/33 (Bonnefon 2016); nine factors, three
clusters, GDP/Rule-of-Law and Gini correlations, German Ethical Rule 9, four
most-spared characters (Awad 2018); 81/72/51 country-level averages and the
relational-mobility association (Awad 2020); α sub-proportionality and the 100%
majority-prevalence equilibrium result (Feffer 2023); the four model names and
the uncompromising-decision finding (Takemoto 2024). No remaining numeric change
required.

## Reviewer 2 — Evidence boundaries and overclaim control

**Final verdict: PASS.** Rendering is authorized by this reviewer.

Required revisions: three boundaries were missing or too weak. The Bonnefon
mandate-backfire claim read as an empirical finding rather than the authors'
reasoned inference from stated preference. The Feffer result needed explicit
framing as a theoretical proof in a stylized two-group model, not an audit of a
deployed system. The Takemoto result needed version-and-prompting instability
stated, not merely implied.

Resolution: all three boundaries are now spoken in the script at the point of
claim and recorded in the notes. The reviewer also confirmed the script never
asserts that the cited correlations are causal, and that the Awad 2018 segment
preserves the authors' own refusal to conclude policy should follow public
opinion. The self-selection limit on both Moral Machine samples is stated on air
rather than only in the notes. No remaining boundary change required.

## Reviewer 3 — Format, disclosure compliance, and listenability

**Final verdict: PASS.** Rendering is authorized by this reviewer.

Required revisions: none outstanding on examination of the revised package.

The reviewer confirmed: strict two-speaker alternation with 43 Host/Rowan turns
and no third voice; the fictional-correspondent and AI-generated disclosure
appears once, early, in a single sentence, with no close-out repetition, matching
the current shortened-disclosure rule; the required complete-citations sentence
appears verbatim in both script and notes; all five manifest source keys carry
numbered citation entries with author, title, venue, year, and direct Zotero
links; the sign-off uses a topic-specific imperative followed by the series
close. Spoken numbers are written for the ear, the "two-sentence abstract" lands
as a genuine sharpening rather than filler, and no turn is long enough to lose a
walking listener.
