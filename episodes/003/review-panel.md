# Episode 003 — review panel

Three independent reviewers read the revised script and notes against the extracted
full text of `arXiv:1803.07170v2` (the authors' preprint of Awad et al., "Drivers
are blamed more than their automated cars when both make mistakes," *Nature Human
Behaviour* 4, 2020, 134–143). Round one returned two failing reviews; seventeen
revisions were applied and the raw round-one findings are preserved in
`review-round1-findings.md`. The verdicts below are the post-revision verdicts.

## Reviewer 1 — fact-checker

Checked every quantitative and bibliographic claim against the preprint: Table 1
coefficients, the three sample sizes (786 / 382 / 586 participants, the latter two
restricted by design to shared-control conditions), observation counts, the three
exclusion filters including the 169-character transcription check, all cell means,
the competence null, residual blame on uninvolved parties, the Guardian Angel
reversal, and the under-reaction and crashworthiness arguments. The published
citation was independently confirmed via Crossref: volume 4, issue 2, pages
134–143, online 28 October 2019, eight authors in the stated order. The
preprint-versus-published disclosure is accurate and sufficient.

Required revisions: Two precision errors, both now corrected. First, the script
described the "made a mistake" coefficient as uniformly enormous across all three
studies; study 3's value is 11.645 (SE 0.939) — significant but an order of
magnitude smaller, because the dilemma framing compresses the scale. The script
and notes now give all three values and name the compression. Second, the Guardian
Angel reversal was called the only case in the paper where the machine draws more
blame than the human; the authors scope that claim to shared control, and
supplemental tables show industry blamed above the user in the fully autonomous car
too. Both documents now scope it to the shared-control cars, where a human is
actually a driver. An optional note on the placement of the study-3 null was also
adopted.

**Final verdict: PASS.**

## Reviewer 2 — evidence boundaries

Assessed the gap between what the vignette studies measured and what the script
implies, with attention to causal language, the survey-to-jury extrapolation, author
attribution, the study-3 non-replication, generalisability, and the labelling of
speculation. The jury extrapolation, the passenger-residual interpretation and the
speculative passages were acceptable as first drafted. Three must-fix findings and
five lesser ones were raised; all are now settled.

Required revisions: The closing asserted a causal mechanism the authors expressly
disclaim — they write that why machine drivers are blamed less "remains an open
question" and offer a different candidate explanation. The closing now states the
measured contrast, flags the mechanism as open, and the conserved-blame "absorbs"
phrasing is gone from all three places it appeared. The claim that a human
supervisor "functions as liability insulation" asserted a legal category from
0–100 rating scales with no liability variable measured; it is now marked as this
show's inference extending the authors' forecast, and the notes record that no
liability outcome was measured. Most seriously, the boundary list credited the
authors with three limits that are not theirs and that they would contest — they
argue their vignettes *do* show external validity and never flag the US-only
sample as a limitation. The list now attributes only the study-3 null to the
authors, claims the other three for the show, and states the authors' contrary
position on air. Additionally: the study-3 placement claim was softened to match
where the null actually appears, the null is now described as not shown rather than
demonstrated absent, population language for measured results is scoped to these
participants and the sample is named in-body at first use, the sensor metaphor is
labelled as the show's framing with the survey-courtroom gap pre-empted, the
Guardian Angel mechanism is attributed as the authors' explanation, and the version
hedge no longer claims more about the published version than the title and abstract
can support.

**Final verdict: PASS.**

## Reviewer 3 — craft and house rules

Judged as audio for an intelligent non-specialist. Confirmed passing on first read:
zero arabic digits in dialogue, one short AI-and-fiction disclosure stated once with
no close-out repeat, the verbatim Zotero show-notes line, a complete spoken author
list for the focal source, the preprint disclosure on air, speculation labelled,
continuity callbacks to episodes 001 and 002, a topic-specific imperative before the
sign-off, and the next-episode tease. No banned phrasing; only Host and Rowan turns.
The single-focal-source structure against the guide's roughly five-article target is
accepted as a deliberate one-paper deep read and is logged as a recorded precedent.

Required revisions: Citation hygiene, now repaired. The turn asserting two outside
empirical findings — algorithm aversion, and robots judged differently from humans —
named no source on air; both are now cited completely in the dialogue and appear in a
new Background sources section in the notes, framed as work the authors cite. The
pre-1960s crashworthiness history is now attributed to the authors' framing with
Nader's *Unsafe at Any Speed* (1965) named, and the German prohibition is attributed
to the Ethics Commission on Automated and Connected Driving's 2017 report for the
Federal Ministry of Transport and Digital Infrastructure; both are in Background
sources. The spoken citation now gives both the online date and the 2020 volume and
pages. A single clause now names the combined blame-and-causal-responsibility
construct that every quoted mean aggregates. The notes runtime estimate was
corrected from fourteen to approximately sixteen minutes and will be reconciled
against the rendered duration.

**Final verdict: PASS.**
